Satellite antennas: Bricohams designs and printable parts

Authors: Anatol Emilio Lopez Maitchovcow EA5KAO, Diego Roca EA5JEF, and Pedro Roca, Bricohams founders and board members. Information: https://bricohams.org and https://bricohams.com.

Antenna section of the Satellite Basics guide. The designs and parts are original to Bricohams: dimensions calculated with a method-of-moments solver written for this project and parts modeled in code. They do not reproduce the antenna projects by PY4ZBZ or PY5LO included in PT2VM's guide, whose permission was granted to that author.

You can hear the ISS with the original antenna on high passes, but working low-power FM satellites usually calls for a hand-aimed directional antenna. A long whip or counterpoise helps somewhat but does not replace a directional antenna.

Handheld dual-band Yagi

Two Yagis share a non-conductive boom: 3 elements for 2 m in the horizontal plane and 5 elements for 70 cm in the vertical plane, rotated 90 degrees. With orthogonal, centered planes, the simulation shows no appreciable cross-band coupling: each Yagi performs the same with or without the other present. Real misalignment of the elements does introduce some coupling.

The design prioritizes gain and front-to-back ratio with SWR below 1,4 across the satellite segment, feeding the driven element directly with 50 Ω coax and no matching network. An SWR of 1,3-1,35 reflects about 2% of the power; this is acceptable for a handheld radio.

Materials

  • 20 mm PVC tube, 1030 mm.
  • 4 mm aluminum rod, about 5,5 m total. Brass rod also works; the measuring

tape requires a different block.

  • Printed parts: 6 element clamps, 2 driven-element blocks, and 1 handle; see STL parts.
  • M3 x 8 screws to secure rods and parts, and M3 x 10 screws with ring terminals for the driven elements.
  • Two lengths of thin 50 Ω coax, such as RG-316 or RG-58, with a common-mode choke on each: several ferrite beads near the block or 4-5 coax turns about 3 cm in diameter. Check the choke with the analyzer.
  • A VHF/UHF diplexer to combine both driven elements into one downlead if you use one radio, such as the UV-K5. With two radios, connect each coax to its own radio.

Assembly

  1. Cut the rods 3-5 mm longer than the table values and file the ends. Put guards on the tips: heat-shrink tubing or caps. It is easy to bring a tip close to your face while turning a handheld antenna.
  2. Mark the x positions on the boom with tape. Install VHF clamps with the rod horizontal and UHF clamps rotated 90 degrees on the boom.
  3. Center the rod in each clamp and tighten it with the top M3 screw. Secure the clamp to the boom with the bottom M3 screw.
  4. In the driven-element blocks, insert each half to the bottom of its hole. The top screw secures it and acts as a terminal: braid on one side and center conductor on the other, with a ring terminal. Seal connections against moisture.
  5. Route coax along the boom toward the reflector, securing it with ties through the block peg, with the choke near the feed point.
  6. Fit the handle between the UHF driven element (231) and UHF director 1 (365), around x ≈ 300. Move it until the antenna is balanced.

Tuning with an analyzer

  • Measure the SWR of each band separately, with the other coax terminated in 50 Ω or connected to the diplexer.
  • If the minimum falls below the segment, shorten the driven element equally on both sides, in 1-2 mm steps. If it falls above, the driven element is too short: replace it.
  • Do not chase an SWR of 1,0: the direct-fed driven element is around 38 Ω by design. What matters is that the minimum falls at 145,9 and 436,5 MHz.
  • Keep away from metal tables and people during measurement. Aim the antenna at the sky.

Horizontal 2 m V dipole

Fixed, non-tracking antenna for listening to the ISS, such as SSTV on 145,800 MHz and APRS on 145,825 MHz, and VHF downlinks. The 100-degree V arms partly fill the nulls of a straight dipole and bring the impedance closer to 50 Ω.

Mount it horizontally on a 20 mm PVC mast, with the printed hub and a common-mode choke on the coax, about 0,5-1 m above the roof or ground. Height above ground changes the real pattern; the simulation is in free space. The Yagi is preferable for transmitting on low passes.

Recommended print settings:

  • Material: PETG or ASA. PLA warps in the sun.
  • Perimeters and infill: 4 perimeters and 40% infill.
  • Layer height: 0,2 mm.
  • Orientation: boom ring horizontal and resting on the flat face of the tab. Print the hub with the socket facing down.

Hole clearances:

  • Boom: 20,4 mm.
  • Rod: 4,3 mm.
  • Direct M3 thread: 2,8 mm.

If your printer closes the holes, change the BOOM, ROD, and M3_TAP constants in generar_stl.py and regenerate the parts.

The script checks that each STL is a single, watertight solid. Volume and dimension data are in piezas.json.

Calculation method and verification

Files in hardware/antenas:

ScriptWhat it does
mom.pyCustom thin-wire method-of-moments solver (Harrington): node currents, segment loads, reduced kernel, and analytically integrated static term
disenar.pyOptimizes dimensions with Nelder-Mead for maximum gain with SWR ≤ 1,3-1,35 and front/back ≥ 15 dB; verifies the dual-band combination and part spacing, and writes resultados.json
generar_stl.pyModels parts with manifold3d and writes STL files and piezas.json
vistas.pyDraws dimensioned plans and part views in docs/atlas

Solver validation against the reference λ/2 dipole:

  • With a 10⁻⁵ λ radius, it gives Z ≈ 78 + j43 Ω, compared with the classic value of 73 + j42,5 Ω. The difference comes from the delta-gap feed.
  • With a 10⁻³ λ radius, the 0,475 λ dipole resonates at 71 − j3,5 Ω.
  • Directivity is 2,15 dBi. Gain calculated from input power agrees with the pattern integral, so power is conserved.

Expected resonant-frequency error: a few percent. That is why the elements are cut long and then tuned.

Model limitations:

  • Free space, without ground or the operator's body.
  • Perfect conductors.
  • Does not model the clamp plastic, which lowers resonance slightly, the boom, coax, or the diplexer.
  • This is not a measurement or performance certificate.

Safety and legality

  • Transmitting requires an amateur radio authorization. Listening does not.
  • With 5 W and an 11 dBi Yagi, do not point at nearby people and keep the elements away from your face while transmitting.
  • Do not use these antennas on unauthorized bands or satellites, such as military UHF satellites.
