Bricohams AZ/EL rotor from the Android app

Integrates the Android app with the rotor of the sister project Brico-Hams/antenaSatCommrotor (ESP32 + two stepper motors + limit switches). Together they make up the complete Bricohams satellite station:

 Satélites (SGP4, TLE, pases) ──► Rotor AZ/EL (ESP32, SGP4 propio) ──► antena
          │                         ▲  Wi-Fi 192.168.4.1 o BLE seguro
          └──► CAT USB (Doppler) ───┼──► radio Quansheng con firmware Bricohams
                                    └── móvil: hora UTC, ubicación, norte (brújula), nivel

The phone acts as the rotor's GPS, clock, compass, and inclinometer, so the station does not need those modules. The rotor follows the pass with its own SGP4 and its safety limiters; the app prepares it, arms it, and watches over it while CAT corrects the radio's Doppler in parallel.

Requirements

  • Rotor with antennaSatCommrotor firmware 0.3.0-prototype or later (API 2, includes the change from hardware/rotor) to use the phone's time. With older firmware (API 1) the app works, but the UTC time must come from the rotor's GNSS.
  • Control token configured over USB on the rotor (24-128 characters) and, for BLE, the rotor's 6-digit PIN.
  • Location configured in Satellites › Settings (or that of the selected pass).

Usage flow

  1. In Satellites, open a pass and tap Rotor AZ/EL: prepare and track this pass. The screen calculates the trajectory (300 points) and compares it with the rotor's mechanics.
  2. Choose Rotor Wi-Fi (connect to its network beforehand; the app forces the traffic over Wi-Fi even if it has no internet) or Secure Bluetooth (pair with the PIN the first time). Enter the token and tap Connect.
  3. Homing: finds the AZ and EL limit switches. After homing the antenna rests at EL 0° pointing at the cable cutoff (mechanical AZ -180°).
  4. Before calibrating, the app indicates which true heading to leave the cable cutoff toward so that the pass does not cross it (middle of the arc of sky that the pass does not visit). Turn the tripod toward that heading.
  5. Calibrate north and level with the phone: rest the phone flat on the boom, with the top toward the elements. The app uses the rotation vector, corrects the magnetic declination with Android's WMM model, and averages the latest readings; it also checks that the phone is level (±1°). You can enter the azimuth by hand if there is no compass or there is metal nearby.
  6. Prepare pass: sends UTC time and location, loads the TLE (only that satellite, id 0), selects it, and moves the antenna to the AOS point.
  7. Track automatically from AOS: the app keeps the screen on and, starting 1 s before AOS, requests /api/track every 2 s. If the rotor replies that the antenna is more than 15° off (acquisition), it re-points it to where the satellite will be and retries. It disarms at LOS or if the rotor enters a fault.
  8. Open CAT on the same pass for the radio's Doppler: both work at the same time.

STOP motors is always at the top and disarms automatic tracking.

Plan warnings

  • Cable: the firmware stops if the mechanical azimuth would cross ±180°. With the rotor calibrated, the app warns and proposes the new cutoff heading.
  • Speed: if the pass demands more °/s than the motor profile, the rotor stops near the maximum. The firmware's esp32_nema17 profile goes from 2.25 to ~5.9°/s.
  • Zenith: above 89° the firmware stops tracking.
  • The percentage of the pass that can be tracked without stops is shown.

Safety

  • Keep the physical emergency stop within reach: the app does not replace it.
  • With BLE, if the link drops while the rotor is moving, the firmware enters fault on purpose; you must Clear fault, repeat homing, and calibrate.
  • The time sent by the app is valid for 10 minutes; as long as the rotor screen is open the app resends it every 60 s. If the rotor has a valid GNSS fix, that takes priority.
  • There is only one owner of the motion: if another client (web, BLE) controls the rotor, the app receives 409 and displays it.

Validation

  • Host tests (Robolectric): RotorTest (frames, reassembly and STOP precedence, limits, TLE, API 1/2 states, client, planner) and RotorActivityTest (connection with simulated transport, prepare pass, stop, errors) and the button from SatelliteActivityTest.
  • Firmware 0.3.0-prototype: esp32 and esp32_nema17 build with PlatformIO.
  • Pending: test with a physical rotor, real Wi-Fi and BLE, and a real phone compass.

Remote rotator

  • The remote console (remote.html, also inside RemoteShackActivity and RemoteStationActivity) shows the rotator panel: read, go to AZ/EL, park and stop, through /api/rotor.
  • Only one client controls the rotator at a time (3 s lease renewed every 1 s); others get rotor_busy. Stop is available to any authenticated client.
  • Closing the page, losing the connection or an expired lease stops the rotator. Each movement is capped at 30 s.
  • Motion is off by default (motion in the rotator config); without it the rotator is read-only.
  • Warning: never transmit without a matched load or antenna.
  • Satellite tracking: in the planner, "Export pass for the rotator (.json)" produces radicodex-pass-track (t/az/el samples, Doppler-independent). The console imports it and only starts moving after the track button is pressed.
  • Speed and limit switches: pendiente de fuente (disabled).
  • Support: Hamlib rotctld with the catalog models (data/remote-station-catalog.json) and the Bricohams BLE/AP protocol on Android. GS-232A/B and SPID ROT2PROG: only in the station agent via --rotor-serial (Hamlib frames, no hardware validation); the BLE/AP rotor is unchanged.
